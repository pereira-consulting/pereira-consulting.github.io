Global | Pillar Two

Pillar Two: side-by-side package moves into domestic law

01

Ireland's Finance (No.2) Bill 2026 (19 October) and a Swedish bill (13 October) implement the OECD side-by-side package of 5 January 2026. Both extend the transitional CbCR safe harbour by a year and add the new side-by-side and substance-based tax incentive safe harbours. Singapore published its list of 18 qualified tax incentives on 14 October. Uruguay issued its domestic top-up tax regulations on 16 October, with returns and payment due 15 months after year end (18 months for the first year, at the tax authority's discretion), and the OECD released version 2.0 of the GloBE information return XML schema, which applies to exchanges from 25 June 2028.

Groups with Singapore incentives may wish to check each one against the published list, since income-based incentives will not qualify. Groups with Uruguayan entities may wish to add the new deadline to their filing calendar.

Uganda | Transfer pricing

Transfer pricing: trade mark ownership is not enough without DEMPE

02

In Mini Bakeries Uganda Ltd v Uganda Revenue Authority (judgment of 21 August 2026), the Tax Appeals Tribunal upheld the disallowance of a 2 per cent royalty paid to a Mauritian trade mark owner that was not shown to perform or control the DEMPE functions. It also rejected interest rates on unsecured intra-group loans that were benchmarked to general bank rates rather than a credit analysis of the borrower.

This is consistent with the OECD Guidelines, which the ATO also applies. It would be sensible to check that the group's functional analysis records who performs and controls each DEMPE function for any IP holding company.

Australia | Part IVA

Australia: ATO view on Part IVA alternative postulates

03

On 21 October 2026 the ATO issued decision impact statements on Hilton International Australia Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Taxation (No 2) [2026] FCA 1325 (under appeal) and Commissioner of Taxation v Hicks [2025] FCAFC 171. The ATO agrees with Hilton that there can be more than one reasonable alternative postulate, that the tax benefit is the highest amount any of them produces, and that an alternative which would itself attract Part IVA is not reasonable. On Hicks, it warns that introducing a trust to fit within that decision may itself form part of a scheme for section 45B.

For any restructure or exit under review, it would be sensible to test the position against every reasonable alternative the ATO could put, not only the most likely one.

Australia | Public CbC

Australia: public CbC reports for calendar year 2025 deferred

04

The ATO has deferred the due date for public country-by-country reports for the period 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2025 from 31 December 2026 to Friday 29 January 2027. The deferral applies automatically.

Registration must be done at least four weeks before the first report is lodged, so it would be sensible to register now if that has not been done.

Pillar Two: filing deadlinesRegistration, returns & payment

Dates are based on the published rules and administrative notices. Confirm against local guidance before relying on them. Uruguay's domestic top-up tax return and payment are due 15 months after year end, which the tax authority may extend to 18 months for the first transitional year. South Africa requires a GMT03 declaration where GloBE information is filed abroad, Kuwait requires every registered group to update its DMTT registration, and Kenya requires a notification of status; none has a published date yet.
