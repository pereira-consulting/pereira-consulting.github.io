Most of the tax problems that surface in a sale process were created years earlier, usually in a restructure that nobody thought of as a tax event at the time.

What changed

Foreign shareholders, and a year of looking back

From 1 October 2026 the principal asset test applies across the 365 days before a CGT event, rather than only at the moment of the event. If more than half the market value of a company’s assets is attributable to Australian real property at any point in that year, a foreign resident holding a non-portfolio interest, broadly 10 per cent or more, may fall within the Australian capital gains net on a sale of those shares.

The amending Act is now law and applies to CGT events on or after 1 October. It also brings in a requirement for certain foreign resident vendors to notify the ATO when they declare that their shares are not indirect Australian real property interests, which is worth building into a sale timetable from the start.

The part worth raising with a client now is that a look back removes the ability to fix the answer by timing completion. If a property holding sat on the balance sheet nine months ago, moving the settlement date would no longer help.

From the runway · Structure

When the step plan and the documents are not the same transaction

Restructures where the plan and the executed documents describe different things come up more often than you would expect. The plan contemplates a scrip for scrip exchange. The documents paper an ordinary share transfer, or a step completes outside the window the rollover depended on. It tends not to be picked up at the time, because the people drafting the documents were not in the tax conversation and the people in the tax conversation never saw what was signed.

Rectification may be available, though it usually needs a court order and does not by itself settle the position with the Commissioner. Either way it is a good deal easier to look at two years out than during diligence, when the buyer’s adviser has read the same documents you have.

Can I see what papered the last restructure, not the advice that recommended it.

The property

What has been on the balance sheet in the last twelve months, not just today.

The register

Who holds ten per cent or more, and where do they file.

The timetable

Where the vendor declaration and the notification sit in the process.

One question to ask

“Can I see the documents that papered your last restructure, rather than the advice that recommended it?”

If the step plan and the executed documents do not describe the same transaction, that is probably work to do now rather than in a process.
